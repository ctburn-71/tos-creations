The Vision — Before Development Began

Revival Creek Homesteader began with a simple but ambitious idea: What if you discovered a nearly forgotten rural town and were given the freedom to bring it back to life?

Not simply by completing missions or following a fixed storyline, but by choosing how you wanted to live.

You might restore an old farmhouse, become a farmer, raise cattle, open a restaurant, operate a grocery store, build homes, or develop a business that helps the community grow.

As more residents arrive, the town would gradually become a functioning community, with interconnected businesses, supplies, property ownership, employment, and an evolving local economy.

Weather, changing seasons, home improvements, vehicles, farming equipment, and eventually opportunities to share the experience with others would help create a world that feels alive.

A town that starts nearly forgotten and becomes whatever the player chooses to make it.

The Weekly Development Journey

Explore the progress, challenges, and milestones as Revival Creek grows from an idea into a playable world.

Week 1 — November 1–7, 2026
Building the World from the Ground Up

November 1 — The First Piece of Land

Development began in Unity with the creation of the first terrain in a new scene named RevivalCreek_Main.

What started as an empty development environment quickly became the beginning of a rural homestead.

The first major additions included:

  • A farmhouse and front porch
  • An old wooden barn
  • A dirt and gravel driveway
  • Initial yard details and fencing
  • Terrain sculpting and rolling countryside
  • Basic lighting and environmental groundwork

The original goal was not to make a collection of buildings. It was to establish a believable place where someone might actually want to live.

The first milestone was complete: Revival Creek had its first homestead.

November 2–3 — Giving the World a Player

Pete, our working player character, was introduced with first-person walking, mouse-look, gravity, and collision handling.

Third-person camera functionality was added so players could experience the world from different perspectives.

Movement and camera testing became an important part of development. We wanted the world to feel natural at ground level, not just look attractive from an overhead Unity camera.

Development expanded toward vehicles, including a pickup truck that players could enter, drive, and view from different camera positions.

November 4–5 — The Town Begins to Take Shape

Attention expanded beyond the farmhouse and barn to the nearby township.

Work introduced the community hardware store, town buildings, roads, access areas, and environmental details.

Community Hardware is the town's permanent supply store. It operates from the beginning and cannot be purchased or taken over by the player.

The hardware store would become a critical part of the town's economy, supplying materials for repairs, improvements, and construction.

Other structures were designed to appear weathered and aged. Only the diner would begin boarded up, awaiting restoration.

November 5–7 — A More Natural Landscape

Development continued with:

  • Expanding and refining rural roads
  • Improving meadows and countryside scenery
  • Creating distant hills and mountain ridges
  • Adding trees and environmental transitions
  • Developing the winding creek and its banks
  • Testing water appearance and vehicle access
  • Refining the town and surrounding countryside

The goal was realism without sacrificing mobile performance.

We also began planning residential development, including additional homes, neighborhood roads, and future population growth.

By the end of Week 1, Revival Creek was becoming a recognizable rural world.

Week 2 — November 8–9, 2026
Turning the Landscape into a Playable Game

The second week focused on an important question: What can players actually do inside Revival Creek?

Entering Community Hardware

The hardware store became an interactive building with a working entrance, interior space, service counter, and shelving.

Door operation was integrated into the existing player interaction controls, establishing the foundation for entering buildings throughout town.

The First Hands-On Construction System

A construction system was introduced, allowing Pete to place and adjust physical objects.

Early work began with shelving and a cabinet before expanding into outdoor fences and gates.

  • Build/Move interface
  • Placement previews
  • Object rotation and finish options
  • Position adjustment and snapping
  • Collision checking and placement restrictions
  • Material costs
  • Undo functionality
  • Save and Load controls
  • In-game instructions

Fence and Gate Construction

Wooden fence sections and a working hinged gate were developed.

Fence sections could connect through endpoint snapping, and the gate could be opened and closed. Work also began on recognizing enclosed boundaries for future pastures and livestock.

The First Working Supply Economy

One of the most significant milestones was connecting construction with Community Hardware.

Instead of receiving unlimited free materials, Pete could now purchase supplies using in-game money.

  • Player wallet
  • Construction timber and fittings
  • Hardware store shopping interface
  • Purchase confirmation and cost calculations
  • Homestead Supply Bay near the barn
  • Purchased-material storage
  • Construction using purchased resources
  • Undo refunds
  • Persistent saving of money and materials

During testing, Pete began with $300 and purchased 36 timber and 10 fittings for $224, leaving $76 in his wallet.

Visit town. Buy materials. Return home. Build something. Save your progress.

Making the Game Easier to Understand

Personal gameplay testing revealed that construction areas were difficult to locate.

Development coordinates and invisible boundaries were replaced with named locations, directional guidance, distance indicators, and visible markers.

Community Hardware, the Homestead Supply Bay, and the Pasture Construction Area became recognizable destinations.

The game should be challenging because of what players want to accomplish, not because simple controls are difficult to understand.

Player-Owned Property

Pete received an initial property measuring approximately 90 × 90 metres, or about two acres.

Ownership and building permissions were separated, allowing construction across suitable property areas while protecting existing structures, trees, driveways, and essential access routes.

Construction was verified in both the Rear Pasture and West Meadow.

Refining the Construction Experience

Personal testing identified difficulties when selecting and moving existing fence panels.

Improvements addressed walking while building, selecting individual pieces, editing position and rotation, saving changes, and preserving construction resources.

By November 9, the corrected selection-to-edit workflow had passed personal gameplay review.

A small usability improvement, but an important achievement for Revival Creek.

In Development or Planned

Planned features are part of the development vision and should not be considered completed gameplay until implemented and tested.

Future Plans

The next major objective is turning the farmhouse into a usable home that Pete can enter, explore, repair, and customize.

Later development will build upon the existing construction, property ownership, and purchasing systems to introduce farming, livestock, additional businesses, and community growth.

Proposed Purchasing Model

One model under consideration is a 14-day free experience followed by a one-time $9.99 permanent unlock, with optional expansion content.

These commercial ideas remain proposals, not final pricing or release commitments.

THE TOS CREATIONS PHILOSOPHY

Simple to Control. Challenging to Build. Rewarding to Master.

Revival Creek is about freedom, creativity, hard work, and opportunity.

The challenge should come from developing a homestead, running a business, managing a farm, earning a living, and helping a forgotten town grow.

The goal isn't simply to rebuild a town. It's to give players the freedom to build a life.
