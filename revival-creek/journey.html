# Revival Creek Homesteader ## From an Idea to a Living Rural World **A TOS Creations Original Game** **Development began: November 1, 2026** **Current status: In Active Development** **Platform focus: Mobile, with future expansion opportunities** ### The Vision — Before Development Began Revival Creek Homesteader began with a simple but ambitious idea: What if you discovered a nearly forgotten rural town and were given the freedom to bring it back to life? Not simply by completing missions or following a fixed storyline, but by choosing how you wanted to live. You might restore an old farmhouse, become a farmer, raise cattle, open a restaurant, operate a grocery store, build homes, or develop a business that helps the community grow. As more residents arrive, the town would gradually become a functioning community, with interconnected businesses, supplies, property ownership, employment and an evolving local economy. Weather, changing seasons, home improvements, vehicles, farming equipment, and eventually opportunities to share the experience with others would help create a world that feels alive. Our guiding vision has remained the same: **A town that starts nearly forgotten and becomes whatever the player chooses to make it.** --- ## Week 1 — November 1–7, 2026 ### Building the World from the Ground Up **November 1 — The First Piece of Land** Actual development began in Unity with the creation of the first terrain in a new scene named `RevivalCreek_Main`. What started as an empty development environment quickly became the beginning of a rural homestead. The first major additions included: - A farmhouse and front porch - An old wooden barn - A dirt and gravel driveway - Initial yard details and fencing - Terrain sculpting and rolling countryside - Basic lighting and environmental groundwork The original goal was not to make a collection of buildings. It was to establish a believable place where someone might actually want to live. **The first milestone was complete: Revival Creek had its first homestead.** ### November 2–3 — Giving the World a Player The next milestone was making the environment explorable. Pete, our working player character, was introduced with a first-person walking controller, mouse-look, gravity and collision handling. Third-person camera functionality was added so players could experience the world from different perspectives. Movement and camera testing became an important part of development. We wanted the world to feel natural at ground level, not just look attractive from an overhead Unity camera. Development also expanded toward vehicles, including a pickup truck that players could enter, drive and view from different camera positions. For the first time, the homestead was becoming a place you could actually explore. ### November 4–5 — The Town Begins to Take Shape Attention expanded beyond the farmhouse and barn to the nearby township. Work introduced and developed the community hardware store, town buildings, roads, access areas and additional environmental details. An important gameplay rule was established: **Community Hardware is the town's permanent supply store. It operates from the beginning and cannot be purchased or taken over by the player.** The hardware store would eventually become a critical part of the town's economy, supplying materials for repairs, improvements and construction. Other structures were intended to look weathered and aged. Only the diner would begin boarded up, awaiting restoration. The pickup also became an increasingly important part of the experience, allowing travel between the homestead and town. ### November 5–7 — Creating a More Natural Landscape The countryside began to receive significant attention. Work included: - Expanding and refining rural roads - Improving the meadow and countryside appearance - Creating more natural distant hills and mountain ridges - Adding trees and environmental transitions - Developing the winding creek and its banks - Testing water appearance and vehicle access near the creek - Refining the overall appearance of the town and surrounding countryside The goal was realism without sacrificing mobile performance. We also began planning residential development, including a winding neighborhood road, additional homes, and future opportunities for population growth. By the end of the first week, Revival Creek was no longer just a farmhouse in an empty field. It was becoming a recognizable rural world with roads, buildings, countryside, a creek, and opportunities for future expansion. --- ## Week 2 — November 8–9, 2026 ### Turning the Landscape into a Playable Game The second development week began with an important transition. Rather than concentrating only on what the world looked like, we focused on **what players could actually do inside it**. ### Entering Community Hardware The hardware store became an interactive building with a working entrance, interior space, service counter and shelving. Door operation was integrated into the existing player interaction controls. This established the foundation for entering buildings and eventually interacting with businesses throughout the town. ### The First Hands-On Construction System We introduced a construction system allowing Pete to place and adjust physical objects. Early construction work began with shelving and a cabinet before expanding into outdoor fences and gates. The system grew to include: - A Build/Move interface - Placement previews - Object rotation and finish options - Position adjustment and snapping - Placement restrictions and collision checking - Material costs - Undo functionality - Save and Load controls - In-game instructions Construction began evolving from a technical placement experiment into an actual gameplay activity. ### Fence and Gate Construction We developed wooden fence sections and a working hinged gate. Fence sections could connect through endpoint snapping, and the gate could be opened and closed by the player. The system also began recognizing connected fence boundaries and enclosed areas, laying the foundation for future pastures and livestock. Different finishes and resource requirements introduced more choice into construction. **The first practical step toward building a working farm was underway.** ### The First Working Supply Economy One of the biggest milestones was connecting construction with the community hardware store. Instead of receiving unlimited free construction materials, Pete could now purchase supplies using in-game money. The first working economy introduced: - A player wallet - Construction timber and fittings - A shopping interface at Community Hardware - Purchase confirmation and cost calculations - A Homestead Supply Bay near the barn - Purchased-material storage - Construction using purchased resources - Material refunds through Undo - Persistent saving of money, supplies, purchases and structures In testing, Pete began with $300 and could purchase 36 timber and 10 fittings for $224. The remaining $76 stayed in his wallet, and materials were deducted as he built. This was an especially important milestone because it connected several systems into one continuous activity: **Visit town. Buy materials. Return home. Build something. Save your progress.** ### Making the Game Easier to Understand Personal gameplay testing revealed something that automated tests could not: the construction areas were difficult to locate. Players were being guided using development coordinates and invisible boundaries. We improved the experience by introducing named locations, directional guidance, distance indicators and visible property markers. The hardware store, Homestead Supply Bay and Pasture Construction Area became recognizable destinations rather than sets of numerical coordinates. Construction feedback was also improved so the player could understand why a location was unavailable. Our approach became increasingly clear: **The game should be challenging because of what the player wants to accomplish, not because simple controls are difficult to understand.** ### The Homestead Becomes Player-Owned Property The next major milestone was moving beyond small construction-testing areas. Pete was given an initial property measuring approximately 90 × 90 metres, or two acres. Ownership and building permissions became separate systems, allowing construction across suitable areas of the property rather than within one tiny square. Named protected areas were established around existing buildings, trees, the driveway, the barn entrance, and essential access routes. Construction was verified in both the Rear Pasture and West Meadow. This was the first major step toward giving players genuine freedom to develop their land. ### Refining the Construction Experience As construction became more flexible, personal testing identified another important issue: moving an existing fence panel was unnecessarily difficult. Development continued to improve walking while building, selecting individual panels, editing their position and rotation, preserving materials and saving changes. A particularly useful debugging session revealed that the interface was allowing a fence to be highlighted without clearly entering the actual editing mode. Distance-related feedback was also being hidden by generic instructions. After identifying the real interaction problem, we corrected the selection-to-edit workflow. The result was an easier way for players to work with individual structures without needing to rebuild an entire fence. By November 9, this issue had passed personal gameplay review. **A small usability improvement, but an important achievement for the kind of game we want Revival Creek to become.** --- ## Development Progress as of November 9, 2026 ### Created and Playable - An initial rural world with rolling terrain and countryside scenery - Farmhouse, barn, driveway and homestead environment - A growing township with multiple structures - Community hardware store with interactive entrance and interior - Rural roads, residential development areas and surrounding landscape - Creek, banks, distant ridges and woodland features - First-person and third-person player movement - Drivable pickup truck with camera options - Basic hands-on construction - Wooden fencing and an interactive gate - Individual fence and gate editing - Player-owned homestead property - Protected construction areas and property boundaries - Player wallet and hardware-store purchasing - Homestead material storage - Construction resource consumption and Undo refunds - Saving, loading and recovery protections - In-game navigation guidance and construction instructions ### In Development or Planned - Functional farmhouse and barn interiors - Home renovations, paint, flooring, furniture and customization - Additional property and land ownership - Larger fences, multiple pastures and livestock handling - Crop planting, gardens, harvesting and farming income - Tractors, implements and additional vehicles - Restoration and operation of the diner and other businesses - Resident housing, population growth and town employment - A more complete supply, production and trading economy - Dynamic weather and seasonal changes - Optional real-world ZIP-code-based weather - Expanded player livelihoods - Future shared-world and multiplayer possibilities These features are part of the development vision; they should not be considered completed gameplay until built and tested. --- ## The Business Plan Revival Creek Homesteader is being designed as a TOS Creations original game with a player-friendly purchasing model. Our current proposed approach is: **14 days free to experience the game, followed by a one-time $9.99 permanent unlock.** There would be no required annual subscription and no automatic charge when the trial expires. Players who choose to purchase would retain their existing world and progress. Optional expansion bundles are also planned for home customization, farming equipment, business improvements, vehicles and additional lifestyle experiences. The goal is to make players want more because they enjoy what the game offers—not because the basic experience is intentionally frustrating or incomplete. These commercial features remain in the planning stage and may be refined before release. --- ## What Comes Next? Our next major development objective is to begin turning the existing farmhouse into a usable home. That means moving from an exterior building to a structure Pete can actually enter, explore, repair and customize. Future construction will build on the property ownership, purchasing and object-placement systems developed during these first two weeks. From there, we can begin introducing more meaningful farming activities, equipment, livestock, businesses and community development. Revival Creek Homesteader is still early in development. Much of the larger gameplay vision remains ahead of us. But in the first nine days of hands-on Unity work, the project has progressed from an empty landscape into a playable rural environment with driving, interactive buildings, purchasing, construction, property ownership and persistent player progress. ### The TOS Creations Philosophy **Simple to control. Challenging to build. Rewarding to master.** Revival Creek is about freedom, creativity, hard work and opportunity. The challenge should come from developing a homestead, running a business, managing a farm, earning a living and helping a forgotten town grow. It should never come from struggling to move a simple fence panel. **The goal isn't simply to rebuild a town. It's to give players the freedom to build a life.** *Revival Creek Homesteader — A TOS Creations Original* *Development Journal — November 9, 2026*
